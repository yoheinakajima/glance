Glance vs Laya Vision

One accuracy×speed score on a laptop, then the suites that make it

Same images, same typed questions, same machine. The headline is a single product score: equal-weight accuracy across every suite we ran, times relative speed, combined as a geometric mean. That score then breaks into the secondary board mix (where the interesting gap is) and the fresh-photo check (contamination-safer, closer).

Unified = √(Accuracy × Speed). Accuracy is the unweighted mean of ten suite accuracies (two fresh + eight secondary), so the secondary mix is not buried under one photo set. Speed is min(p50) / p50 on the dog.jpg 3-question batch (warmup excluded). Cost is not a third axis here: both systems run on-device with no API bill, so relative wall time is the cost proxy and is already in Speed.

Laya wins the unified score because it is much faster; Glance wins the accuracy axis (0.886 vs 0.689). Dropping the pets37 suite (where Laya’s breed keys collapsed to 0.175) raises Laya’s unified score to 0.864 and barely moves Glance (0.518) — the pets miss is real and left in the headline accuracy. This is a product comparison, not a matched-backbone bakeoff. Laya was run outside Glance (Glance only runs Apache-2.0/MIT weights). Laya’s published L4 ~41 ms is not claimed here.

5 Limits

  • One machine (Apple M5). The score will move on other chips and runtimes.
  • Equal suite weight is a presentation choice: it elevates the secondary mix by design. An n-weighted accuracy is Glance 0.888 / Laya 0.723 — same qualitative story.
  • Mini slices on secondary boards (n≈30–50). Enough to shape a score; not enough for tight ranking CIs. ECE deferred.
  • Older VQA boards may favour whoever saw them in training; fresh-photo remains the contamination-safer check.
  • No claim that local official minis match Laya’s published HF percentages.
  • Stock JevBench is text-only; the two-axis geometric mean here is in that spirit, for vision, on this laptop only.
