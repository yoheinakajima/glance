# Score lab notebook

Branch `score-lab`, started 2026-09-20. Live notebook for the rating-scale experiments. Entries are appended in order
and never rewritten after the fact; corrections are new entries. Everything here is zero-training: prompts, logit
readout, and a handful of calibration numbers fit on a calibration split.

## 2026-09-20 Entry 1: why this experiment (written before any lab result existed)

**Starting point.** In the v0 full evaluation (`STATUS.md`, run `20260920T002639Z-2091d3`) the `score` question type
was the weakest result: on `blur_ladder` (4 levels, Caltech-101 images, n = 250 test) Qwen3-VL-4B reached accuracy
0.496, MAE 0.67 levels, calibrated ECE 0.149. The confusion pattern was systematic rather than random: level 2 -> 3
(51), 0 -> 1 (49), 1 -> 2 (10). The model orders images correctly and places every boundary one step too high. An
offline refit on the saved logits with one bias per level plus a temperature (4 numbers, 250 labeled items) lifted
accuracy to 0.724 and MAE to 0.37, but ECE stayed at 0.15 (`tools/analyze_score_offsets.py`).

**Diagnosis.** The v0 `score` method asks about each level in isolation ("Candidate answer: <level text>. Is this
candidate the correct answer?") and never shows the scale, by design (HANDOFF section 6: "level numbers and
neighbors are never shown"). A level description such as "slightly soft" has no meaning without its neighbours, so
each yes/no judgment is made against an implicit, uncalibrated reference. That is a property of how the question
is asked, not necessarily of what the model can see.

**Hypotheses (registered before collecting data).**
- H1. Showing the whole scale and asking threshold questions ("is it step k or higher?", method `cumulative`) moves
  the boundaries to the right place, because every judgment is now relative to explicit neighbours. Expect a large
  accuracy gain over `independent` at equal calibration effort.
- H2. Reading the next-token logits over the digits of a numbered scale (`digits`) does about as well as
  `cumulative` at a quarter of the cost (1 forward pass instead of K-1 or K).
- H3. Adding three reference images of known level to the request (`anchors_*`; the API already accepts 4 images)
  helps most where the verbal descriptions are vaguest (mild levels of noise and JPEG artifacts).
- H4. Per-level calibration (one bias per level + temperature, or Platt scaling per threshold) is needed on top of
  any readout to reach ECE <= 0.05; a single temperature is not enough for ordinal scales.

**Bar for calling the result impressive** (fixed now): accuracy >= 0.85, MAE <= 0.25 levels and ECE <= 0.05
(15 equal-mass bins) on the held-out test split, on at least 4 of the 5 scales, for one single method.

## 2026-09-20 Entry 2: test scales

v0's only `score` suite used Caltech-101 images, which are small and already soft, so "level 0 = sharp" was partly
mislabeled. The lab uses five synthetic degradation ladders on sharper source photos, generated by
`glance/lab/ladders.py` (parameters in its `LADDERS` dict; manifests in `lab/manifests/`; preview sheets in
`lab/sheets/`).

- Source pool: Oxford-IIIT Pet test images (CC BY-SA 4.0, pinned in `DATASETS.md`) that the `pets37` suite does not
  use: 2,669 candidates -> 2,416 with longest side >= 448 px -> 1,691 above the 30th percentile of Laplacian-variance
  sharpness (threshold 533.6) -> 1,611 with mean luminance >= 70. Nothing is upscaled; every image is resized to a
  448 px longest side before degradation.
- Five ladders, 4 levels each, level 0 = untouched base image: `blur` (Gaussian radius 0 / 1.2 / 3.0 / 7.0),
  `noise` (additive Gaussian sigma 0 / 10 / 25 / 60), `jpeg` (quality 95 / 25 / 10 / 3), `exposure` (gain 1.0 / 0.5 /
  0.25 / 0.1), `resolution` (downscale factor 1 / 3 / 6 / 12, then bicubic back up).
- 1,000 items per ladder, 125 per (split, level); split = calibration if (i // 4) is even, else test; 3 anchor
  source images per ladder are reserved and never used as items. Each ladder samples the pool with its own seed.
- Level texts describe situations, not numbers (see `LADDERS`). I looked at all five preview sheets before freezing
  the parameters: every level is visibly distinct from its neighbours; the subtlest steps are noise 0 -> 1
  (sigma 10) and jpeg 0 -> 1 (quality 25), which matches their descriptions ("fine speckle", "when you look
  closely"). `blur` and `resolution` look alike at a glance but are separate scales with separate questions.
  Parameters frozen as listed.

## 2026-09-20 Entry 3: methods, calibration and the rules that keep the test split clean

Method definitions and exact prompt text: `glance/lab/score_methods.py` (lab prompt version `s1`; the harness's `p1`
templates are untouched).

| Method | Forward passes (K = 4) | What the model sees | Readout |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `independent` (v0) | 4 | one level text at a time, scale never shown | yes/no logit per level, softmax |
| `cumulative` | 3 | the whole scale, numbered from 1; "is the answer step k or higher?" | yes/no logit per threshold -> P(level >= k) -> level distribution (made monotone) |
| `digits` | 1 | the whole scale, numbered from 0; "answer with the number" | logits over the digit tokens, softmax |
| `anchors_cumulative` | 3 | as `cumulative`, plus 3 reference images of known level | same |
| `anchors_digits` | 1 | as `digits`, plus 3 reference images | same |

Anchors are one reserved photo shown at three levels, passed as extra request images `ref<level>` before the image
being rated; the instructions name them by backticked id. With 4 images the 768-token budget gives each image 192
tokens. Which three levels to show is a design choice with no obvious answer, so the pilot compares 1-2-3, 0-1-2 and
0-1-3.

Calibrations compared per method: raw; single temperature (what v0 ships); one bias per level + temperature (4
numbers); for the cumulative methods, Platt scaling per threshold (6 numbers).

Rules:
1. Pilots and every design choice (which anchors, which calibration, which method is "the winner") use the
   CALIBRATION split only: fit on its first half, judge on its second half (`glance.lab.analyze --dev`).
2. For each method the calibration variant is chosen by NLL on the fit data, never on the evaluation data.
3. The test split is evaluated once, for the final table.
4. Experiments run on the prefix-cached path for speed (3.75x, 100/100 identical decisions in the v0 acceptance
   check, but max |dz| 0.075 vs the 0.05 limit, see `STATUS.md` M4). The winning method is then re-run on the
   reference path and both numbers are reported.
5. Logits are saved per item and method (`lab/runs/*.jsonl`), so every number can be recomputed offline.

## 2026-09-20 00:30 Entry 4: first pilot reading (blur only, DEV split, n = 80 judged) and two additions

Pilot command: `python -m glance.lab.collect --out lab/runs/pilot.jsonl --split calibration --limit 160
--anchors "0:123,0:012,0:013" --prefix-cache` (calibration split only; 160 items per scale; fit on the first 80,
judged on the last 80). Measured cost per item on the cached path: `independent` 0.46 s, `cumulative` 0.35 s,
`digits` 0.16 s, `anchors_cumulative` 1.1 s, `anchors_digits` 0.21 s (4 images, 555 image tokens).

Blur, DEV, after the first analysis (`lab/runs/pilot_partial_blur.md`):
- Every readout ranks images almost perfectly whatever its accuracy: Spearman between expected score and true level
  is 0.84-0.96 for all nine method variants, raw or calibrated.
- Raw accuracy is poor everywhere (0.46-0.66) and a single temperature never changes it (it cannot move an argmax).
  Per-level calibration is what moves accuracy: `independent` 0.662 -> 0.900 (MAE 0.13, ECE 0.031, floor 0.052),
  `cumulative` 0.575 -> 0.787, `digits` 0.463 -> 0.600, best anchor variant `anchors_digits@0:013` 0.650 -> 0.887.
- So on blur H1 is NOT supported: showing the scale and asking threshold questions did not beat the v0 readout once
  both get per-level calibration. H4 is supported. The v0 failure on `blur_ladder` (0.496) now looks like two things
  stacked: soft source images that made "level 0 = sharp" partly wrong, and a missing per-level offset.
- Caveat: n = 80, one scale. No conclusion yet; four scales still running.

Additions made after this reading (both zero-training, both standard calibration practice):
1. `matrix` calibration (matrix scaling: softmax(W x' + b) on standardized logits, L2 = 0.05), for every readout.
2. The calibration variant for each method is now chosen by 5-fold cross-validated NLL on the fit data instead of
   plain fit-data NLL, so a more flexible calibration cannot win by overfitting. The evaluation data still plays no
   part in the choice.
Follow-up analyses added in `glance/lab/extras.py`: learning curve (labeled examples needed), cross-scale transfer of
a calibration, and an ensemble of readouts. Early blur learning curve on DEV: 8 labeled examples 0.75, 16 -> 0.84,
32 -> 0.87 (mean of 20 random draws).

## 2026-09-20 00:45 Entry 5: side check on multiple choice, and a second method family (magnified view)

**Side check, no GPU (`tools/analyze_choice_ensemble.py`, output in `results/v0/analysis/`).** Question: multiple
choice trails Claude Opus 5 by 4-5 points; does combining the two local backends close it? Both scored every option
of every item in the full v0 run, so the combination (weighted sum of temperature-scaled log-probabilities; two
temperatures and the weight fit on the calibration split) can be tested on saved logits. Test split: pets37 0.952
(Qwen 0.892, SigLIP2 0.956, Opus 0.932), caltech101 0.937 (Qwen 0.919, SigLIP2 0.932, Opus 0.968). So the combination
does not beat the better single model on accuracy. It does calibrate very well (ECE 0.015 and 0.026, floors 0.014 and
0.019) and reaches selective accuracy 0.990 / 1.000 at 80% coverage. Half of Qwen's caltech101 errors are the
dataset's own `Faces` vs `Faces_easy` pair (9 of 18). Decision: no further GPU time on multiple choice tonight.

**Second pilot reading (blur and noise complete, DEV, n = 80 judged each; jpeg partial).** Noise: every method lands
between 0.675 and 0.775 after calibration, Spearman about 0.9, within-one-level 0.975-1.0. Blur: 0.64-0.90. Anchors
help a little on noise (0.74-0.78 vs 0.68-0.73 without) and not on blur, at 3-5x the cost. With 80 fit items the
richer calibrations are data-starved, so the full run (500 fit items per scale) should be somewhat better, but the
pattern is clear: ranking is good everywhere, and the residual errors sit at boundaries defined by fine detail
(clean vs light grain, no artifacts vs mild artifacts).

**Diagnosis.** That is a perception limit more than a calibration limit: sigma-10 grain or quality-25 JPEG blocks are
pixel-scale structure in a 448 px image, and nothing in the prompt makes the model attend to it.

**New method family `zoom_*` (registered before running it).** Same three readouts, but the request carries a second
image `zoom`: the centre 1/3 x 1/3 of `img0` enlarged 3x with nearest-neighbour sampling, so grain, 8x8 compression
blocks and resampling steps keep their structure and simply get bigger. The instructions gain one sentence saying
what `zoom` is. Still zero-training and still inside the existing request format (2 of the 4 allowed images; each
gets 384 image tokens). Hypothesis H5: `zoom_*` raises accuracy most on noise and jpeg, a little on blur and
resolution (the 0 -> 1 boundary), and not at all on exposure. Example: `lab/sheets/_zoom_example.jpg`.
Pilot 2 runs the three `zoom_*` methods on the same 160 calibration items per scale as pilot 1.
